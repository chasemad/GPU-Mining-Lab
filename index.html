01 / The beginning

Started from individual components

I selected components and assembled the first rig myself: GPUs, motherboards, power supplies, risers, storage, cooling hardware, networking, and an open-air frame. I learned by building and bringing each system online.

02 / Expansion

One rig became a multi-system setup

I expanded the hardware and managed the workers using HiveOS. A saved monitoring screenshot captures four workers and 29 GPUs, with the system status and power information visible at that point in time.

03 / Operations

Troubleshooting was the real learning

GPUs sometimes unexpectedly disconnected or became unstable. I inspected connections and components, made adjustments, and retested. High heat in a residential space also required dedicated ventilation and ducted exhaust.

Photo documentation

The gallery shows actual build photographs, not generated illustrations. Monitor details and identifiable HiveOS worker names are obscured in the prepared public copies.

Real monitoring and troubleshooting records

Saved HiveOS screenshots record individual GPU temperatures, fan speeds, power readings, and one GPU driver-error notification with memory temperature readings around 102–106°C. These are historical observations from the project, not recommended operating settings.

04 / Operations

Working through hardware and thermal failures

The difficult part was not just getting a rig to boot. I had to monitor real behavior, investigate GPU dropouts, check connections, adjust configuration, and retest stability.
